Our research on reliable alerting for time-series anomaly detection under changing conditions gets presented today. The purpose is a calm ten-minute talk with ten to fifteen slides, composed answers, and classroom submission of the presentation and ten-to-fifteen-page project report before the viva, exactly as the [Guidelines for Report](../Guidelines%20for%20Report.md) requires. The anchor is 10 AM onwards. The team confirms the classroom deadline, channel, and venue ahead on Day 11 from the notified instructions rather than assuming any detail.

## Who carries what into the room

Aman carries the offline deck and headline figures with a shared copy as fallback, and speaks to the pipeline and reproducibility story. Pratyush carries the single spiral-bound supervisor-signed group report and holds the decision narrative and timekeeping. Nakul coordinates the classroom submission and speaks to metrics and audit with the technical viva lead.

Each student confirms their own classroom submission of presentation and project report before the viva, which is separate from the one physical group report copy. Contingencies stay practical: a corrupted deck falls back to the shared offline copy, and a missing submission confirmation is rechecked in the room against the notified channel. Nothing here substitutes for acceptance or a mark.

Questions asked are captured while fresh. The submitted group copy stays with the institution per the classroom submission requirement; the team retains its digital report source, slides, traces, and the feedback log for the continuing research. September's Phase-I mid-semester showing continues as research beyond today; it is not handed over as finished. The later Phase-II evaluation follows its own criteria, and no outcome today guarantees a mark or publication.
