# Day 10, Monday 28 September: final proofreading, approval chain, and binding

Our research on reliable alerting for time-series anomaly detection under changing conditions turns into a deliverable today. The purpose is narrow: finish final proofreading and essential fixes, lock an approved report PDF, obtain the supervisor signature on that final exact version, spiral-bind the single group copy, and lock the ten-minute deck with reliable offline files. The day runs about three focused hours per person including collaboration, travel, and waiting time, so errands are split rather than loaded onto one teammate.

Why Monday matters is physical. Printing and signing have turnarounds the team does not control. An optional single-page trial print that catches margins, figure legibility, and page count early in the day is cheaper than a reprint at closing time.

## Change only genuine errors, defer the rest

The prerequisites are the Day 9 rerun and audit notes, the supervisor feedback log with dispositions, the frozen configs and results under the proposed `research/` root, and the corrected draft plus slide skeleton. No new policy, stream, or held-out run enters today.

Aman owns code-adjacent fixes within a tight boundary. Only genuine implementation errors against the frozen intent qualify, such as a miswired config or a figure reading the wrong column. Requested method changes or new test choices are deferred as future work, not implemented as corrections. If a fix touches rows connected to an exposed target, the exposure is disclosed and the result is not claimed as clean. A fix that exceeds the day's time is withdrawn rather than rushed, with the report stating the affected result as unavailable. He also finalizes the existing Day 8/9 deck and its offline essentials rather than building a new deck from scratch.

Pratyush exports and checks the final PDF and confirms approval: he reruns one headline command from the snapshot to confirm draft numbers match, verifies the print-ready PDF against the Guidelines, and confirms the approved version before any signature or binding. Nakul verifies every changed claim traces to a result file, primary citations stay within the seven allowed platforms with background clearly separated, limitations stay explicit, and the research direction stays open with all policy baselines remaining complete comparisons rather than narrowed claims. As his main logistics job he coordinates printing, signature, and binding through the agreed route, with slide technical checks kept brief.

## Lock the exact PDF, then sign it, then bind it

The physical order is fixed: approved final PDF first, then the appropriate supervisor signature on that final exact version, then spiral binding as the institution arrangement. Where the supervisor prefers a digital approval before a physical signature, or a signature on the printed page itself, the team follows that specific variant and records it. No venue or signature form is invented here. Printing an unapproved draft for signature, or editing silently after binding, breaks the chain.

The team verifies the print-ready PDF against the [Guidelines for Report](../Guidelines%20for%20Report.md): cover with project title, supervisor and team names, and logo, then contents, introduction, motivation, literature, problem, objectives and methodology, simulation platform and requirements, conclusion, and references. The group confirms the ten-to-fifteen-page single group copy requirement against the Guidelines. Whether front matter counts inside that range is ambiguous, so the team confirms the interpretation rather than assuming one, and the optional trial page checks margins and readability before the final print. One group copy is the deliverable.

## A deck that fits ten minutes without overloading one owner

Pratyush leads the timed run with speaker handoffs, trimming whatever overruns. Nakul gives technical help where a slide number needs tracing to the result book, and checks slide count and timing. Aman packs reliable offline files of the existing deck and headline figures so saved runs carry the evidence without depending on a network or live run. A second full rehearsal, if wanted, moves to Day 11.

If signing or binding slips, the fallback reserves Day 11 explicitly for those two items with no new technical work.
