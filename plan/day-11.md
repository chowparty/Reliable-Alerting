# Day 11, Tuesday 29 September: buffer, timed rehearsal, and viva readiness

Our research on reliable alerting for time-series anomaly detection under changing conditions is done changing today. The purpose of this last full day is logistics buffer and rehearsal: confirm the bound report position, test the deck where it will run or on a faithful equivalent, and make sure each teammate can defend the whole study beyond their own tasks. The signed bound copy is expected by now, but a pending signature or binding carry from Monday is treated as the first job, not a broken prerequisite. Only slide or source typo fixes and rehearsal-driven slide cuts are allowed; any alteration to the final signed report needs reapproval and a new version, with no silent mismatch between the signed copy and the files. The day runs about three focused hours per person including collaboration and travel, ending early by agreement so the team rests.

Why a real buffer matters is simple. A missing signature, a broken figure, or a talk that runs twelve minutes is fixable on Tuesday and embarrassing on Wednesday. Bounded rehearsal plus tangible viva preparation is the highest-value work left.

## Answer the hard questions with saved evidence

Nakul leads the defence sheet from the audit and final draft: the leakage story, actual alert volume alongside coverage, episode grouping with denominators, the retrospective failure summary with its one-to-two-stream limit, and the unclaimed items including unverified literature features and unmeasured budgets. The defer band and any streak reading appear only if actually tested, with ambiguity kept intact rather than presented as a required method.

The team drills discrete prompts aloud:

- Why is the rule not merely alerting less?
- What did frozen development show versus the held-out run?
- How does chronology protect the comparison?
- How does ranking differ from policy choice, and resource cost differ from alert volume?
- Where does the literature comparison leave novelty, and what do negative results mean here?

Saved runs carry the evidence; no live demo is necessary and none is load-bearing.
